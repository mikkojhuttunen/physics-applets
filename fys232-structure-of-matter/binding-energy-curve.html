How this connects to the lecture
  • 3.2: BE is the energy needed to pull all nucleons apart, BE = (Z mH + N mn − M)c². The curve rises steeply for light nuclei (more strong-force bonds per nucleon), peaks near A ≈ 60 (⁵⁶Fe, ⁵⁸Fe, ⁶²Ni) and falls slowly toward heavy nuclei (Coulomb repulsion).
  • 3.6: A reaction releases Q = ΣBEout − ΣBEin (free nucleons have BE = 0, so they drop out). Moving up the curve releases energy: fusion on the left of the peak, fission on the right.
  • 3.7: In neutron activation analysis the (n,γ) energy is the neutron separation energy of the product nucleus, Sn = BE(A+1) − BE(A). The α particle in an Am-241 smoke alarm is emitted because ⁴He sits far above its neighbours on the curve, which makes Qα positive for heavy nuclei.
  • The curve uses the lightest (most stable) isobar at each A, the “line of stability” of the lecture. Masses are atomic masses; electron binding energies are neglected, as in the lecture. Fragment and product energies are prompt Q-values; delayed β− chains add more.
  • Energy per kilogram of fuel: ²³⁵U fission (200 MeV per nucleus) ≈ 8.2×10¹³ J/kg; a single D+D step ≈ 0.9×10¹⁴ J/kg; D+T ≈ 3.4×10¹⁴ J/kg; complete catalysed deuterium burning (6D → 2⁴He + 2p + 2n) ≈ 3.5×10¹⁴ J/kg.
