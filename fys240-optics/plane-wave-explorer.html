What am I looking at?

Blue = wave crest, red = wave trough. The stripes are surfaces of constant phase (wavefronts) — in 2D they're lines, always perpendicular to the arrow (the wave-vector k). kz controls how tightly the stripes pack along the horizontal (propagation) axis; kx controls the tilt and the vertical spacing. With kx = 0 the wave travels exactly along z and the wavefronts are vertical. Increasing kx relative to kz tilts the fronts and bends the direction of travel away from z.

What am I looking at?

The white arrow is the wave-vector k = (kx, ky, kz). The tilted, moving bands are surfaces of constant phase (wavefronts) — flat planes perpendicular to k, marching along it at the phase velocity. The three translucent cross-sections through the origin show the actual field value on each coordinate plane: the x–y face reveals the stripe pattern set by kx and ky, the x–z face by kx and kz, and the y–z face by ky and kz. Set one component to zero to see a face go "flat" (uniform along that pair of axes).
