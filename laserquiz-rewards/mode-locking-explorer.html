Mode-Locking Explorer

Two views of the same idea. First: why locking the phases of many cavity modes together produces a short pulse instead of noise. Second: a real (simplified) simulation of how a laser finds that locked state on its own, starting from nothing but noise — the same physics behind SESAM and Kerr-lens mode-locking (KLM), switchable via the absorber-recovery-time control.

Below a threshold (loss-dependent) the cavity never builds up above noise at all — try dragging this down.
Low = broad gain bandwidth (Ti:sapphire-like), high = narrow (Nd:YAG-like). Narrow gain bandwidth puts a floor under how short the pulse can get, same idea as Tab 1's envelope width.
0 = an idealized instantaneous saturable absorber (this is effectively what Kerr-lens mode-locking provides — no real absorption, just an intensity-dependent spatial loss). Larger values mimic a real SESAM's finite carrier-recombination recovery.

Model & approximations (Tab 2): single polarization, single transverse mode, scalar field on a 256-point time grid, one round trip = one split-step update (gain + gain-bandwidth filtering + dispersion applied in the frequency domain via FFT; SPM and the saturable absorber applied pointwise in time). Units throughout are arbitrary/normalized, chosen for clear qualitative behavior rather than matched to a specific real cavity. A small persistent noise term is added every round trip, standing in for spontaneous emission — this is also what makes the simulation self-starting rather than needing a manually seeded pulse. Passive mode-locking, active mechanisms (e.g. acousto-optic modulators) not modeled.
