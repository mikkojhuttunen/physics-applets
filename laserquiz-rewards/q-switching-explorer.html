Q-Switching Explorer

Store inversion in the gain medium while the cavity is held lossy, then suddenly open the Q. Watch the giant pulse build up and dump the stored energy — and see what happens when the switch isn't fast enough.

Real Pockels-cell / AOM switches are a few to a few tens of ns and barely register here — the buildup itself takes hundreds of ns, so a switch has to be genuinely sluggish (approaching this slider's upper end) before it visibly degrades the pulse. Multi-microsecond switch times would show a much bigger effect, but no real active Q-switch technology is that slow, so that regime isn't offered.

Model & approximations: the hold-off (pumping) phase itself is not simulated — you set the stored inversion directly as a multiple of the final-Q threshold. From t = 0 the cavity loss ramps linearly from the hold-off value to the final value over τswitch, and the coupled photon-density / inversion rate equations are integrated numerically (RK4) with a small fixed spontaneous-emission source term seeding the build-up from noise. Single spatial mode, single pass, no saturable-absorber (passive) Q-switching — active switching only. Gain-medium parameters (σ, upper-level lifetime, ion density, mode volume, output coupling) are shared with the CW rate-equation applet's Nd:YAG / ruby presets.
