Effective index method

The cross-section is split into the region under the strip/rib/core (|x| < w/2) and the region outside it. Each region is a 1D layer stack in y; its slab modes give effective indices Nin and Nout from the exact transfer-matrix dispersion relation. A lateral three-layer slab Nout | Nin (width w) | Nout is then solved with the other polarisation (quasi-TE: TE vertically, TM laterally). The mode field is approximated by the product Vin(y)·L(x).

2D finite-difference reference

The semi-vectorial wave equation for the dominant field component is discretised on a non-uniform grid that is fine inside the core and graded outwards, with the field set to zero at the window edge. For quasi-TE (Ex dominant):

∂/∂x [ (1/n²) ∂(n²Ex)/∂x ] + ∂²Ex/∂y² + k₀²n²Ex = β²Ex, Neff = β/k₀

and the same with x and y exchanged for quasi-TM (Ey). The largest eigenvalues are found by shift-and-invert Arnoldi iteration. Modes of the outside slab that only exist because of the finite window are discarded (N must exceed the outside slab index of the same polarisation). The 2D solver runs in the background; the dispersion plot gets 2D points at 14 wavelengths.

ncl = max(nsub, nsup) and t is the film or core thickness. Material dispersion enters ng through N(λ). The grey b–V curves are the analytic asymmetric-slab TE modes for the film alone: channel modes lie below them because lateral confinement costs propagation constant. The single-mode window is where each shown polarisation has exactly one guided (non-leaky) EIM mode. Sellmeier data: fused silica (Malitson 1965), Si₃N₄ (Luke et al. 2015), LiNbO₃ (Zelmon et al. 1997), Si (Salzberg and Villa 1957), GaAs (Marple 1964, approximate), PMMA (Sultanova et al. 2009), SU-8 (Cauchy fit).
