Model and conventions

Lossless plane-parallel etalon with identical surfaces, r = r′ and R = |r|², |r|² + |t|² = 1, illuminated by a plane wave. θt is the propagation angle inside the medium; the round-trip phase difference between successive transmitted partial waves is δφ = (2πν/c)·2n d cos θt, and dispersion of n is neglected. The k-th transmitted partial wave has field amplitude (1−R)RkE₀ relative to the incident field (tt′ = 1 − R); the constant propagation phase common to all of them is dropped. Summing the geometric series gives the Airy function T = 1/[1 + 4R/(1−R)² sin²(δφ/2)], derived in FYS.240 Optics, Ch. 8.

The ray schematic is qualitative: the zig-zag angle is exaggerated to at least 14° so that the beams separate, and brightness shows amplitude relative to the first transmitted beam.

FWHM: T = ½ requires sin²(δφc/2) = (1−R)²/(4R). The exact full width is Δνc = ΔνFSR·(2/π)·arcsin[(1−R)/(2√R)], which exists only when Tmin < ½, i.e. R > 3 − 2√2 ≈ 0.172. Linearising sin gives the slide result Δνc ≈ ΔνFSR(1−R)/(π√R) and F = π√R/(1−R).

Stored light: each round trip multiplies the intensity by R², so the intensity falls to 1/e after Ne = 1/(−2 ln R) round trips, and the photon lifetime is τ = Ne/ΔνFSR. For R → 1, Ne ≈ 1/[2(1−R)] and F ≈ 2πNe, equivalently Δνc ≈ 1/(2πτ).

Scans: the horizontal axis is the offset of an etalon resonance from ν₁ in units of ΔνFSR (or Δνc); the small change of ΔνFSR during the scan is neglected. The resolution criterion used is Δνs ≥ Δνc; for two equal Lorentzians this leaves a dip to about 83 % of the maximum between them.
