Tight-Binding Explorer

Energy bands from atomic orbitals and hopping: a 1D chain, two atoms per cell, graphene and h-BN with doping, an fcc s-band, and a group 2 chain with s and p orbitals.

Band E(k) with the N allowed k-points

DOS (states / eV / atom)

What is going on

Each atom contributes one orbital. Electrons sit on-site with energy ε₀ and hop to a neighbour with amplitude −t (booklet §7.4.1, Eq. 7.4.37). Plane waves diagonalise the Hamiltonian:

E(k) = ε₀ − 2t cos(ka) − 2t′ cos(2ka)

The dots are the allowed k-values of a ring of N atoms (periodic boundary conditions). Each holds two electrons. Filled dots are occupied, half-filled dots hold one electron.

  • The bandwidth is 4t. Hopping grows with orbital overlap, so squeezing the lattice widens the band.
  • Near the band bottom E ≈ E₀ + ħ²k²/2m*, with m* = ħ²/(2ta²): a narrow band means heavy electrons.
  • The 1D density of states diverges at the band edges (van Hove singularities, §8.2.1).

Try this

  • Step N from 4 to 40 and watch the discrete levels merge into a continuous band.
  • Set 1 electron per atom (half-filled band: metal), then 2 electrons per atom (full band: insulator). Real divalent metals such as Mg still conduct because the s and p bands overlap (HW6, Q3; see the s + p tab).

Bands E(k) in the first Brillouin zone

DOS (states / eV / cell)

What is going on

With two atoms A and B per unit cell the Bloch Hamiltonian is a 2×2 matrix. The hopping alternates between t₁ (inside the cell) and t₂ (between cells):

E±(k) = ± √( Δ² + t₁² + t₂² + 2t₁t₂ cos ka ), Δ = (ε_A − ε_B)/2

The bands are separated by a gap at the zone edge, k = π/a:

E_g = 2 √( Δ² + (t₁ − t₂)² )

  • Unequal atoms (Δ ≠ 0) give an ionic solid like NaCl. Unequal bonds (t₁ ≠ t₂) give a covalent gap, as in polyacetylene.
  • Set t₂ = 0: the chain falls apart into isolated dimers, and the two flat bands at ±t₁ are the bonding and antibonding levels of the H₂ molecule from §2.3.
  • An insulator needs a gap and an even number of electrons per cell. The absorption edge is at λ = 1240 nm·eV / E_g, which is the threshold for optical transitions of Chapter 7.

Try this

  • Start from the uniform chain (Δ = 0, t₁ = t₂). This is the band of Tab 1 folded back into a zone of half the size, with no gap.
  • Load the "Odd electron count" preset: the gap is there, but the Fermi level cuts through a band and the material is a metal.
  • Swap t₁ and t₂: the bulk bands are identical. The difference shows up only at the chain ends, which is the starting point of topological insulators (SSH model).

π-bands along Γ – M – K – Γ

DOS (states / eV / cell)

Upper band E₊(kx, ky) over the plane. Light colours are high energy. The dark spots at the six corners of the hexagonal Brillouin zone are the Dirac points K and K′, where the two bands touch. Dashed triangle: the path in the band plot. When the material is doped, the white curve is the Fermi contour of the occupied pocket around K and K′.

What is going on

The honeycomb lattice has two atoms per cell, one on each sublattice. One π-orbital per atom gives

E±(k) = ± √( Δ² + t² |f(k)|² ), f(k) = 1 + eik·a₁ + eik·a₂

with on-site energies ±Δ. In graphene Δ = 0 and f vanishes at K, so the bands touch linearly, E = ±ħv_F|δk| with v_F = √3·t·a / 2ħ (booklet §8.3.1). In h-BN the two sublattices are different atoms (B and N), which gives Δ ≠ 0 and a gap 2Δ. It is the same mechanism as the ionic chain in Tab 2.

Doping shifts the Fermi level. The band has 4 states per cell (2 bands, 2 spins), so neutral graphene has 2 electrons per cell and the lower band is exactly full. Changing that number moves E_F up or down; near the Dirac point E_F = ħv_F√(π|n|), where n is the extra carrier density. Gating and chemical doping reach about 10¹³ cm⁻², which is only 0.01 electrons per cell.

The saddle points at M produce the two peaks of the DOS at ±√(Δ² + t²): van Hove singularities of a 2D band.

Try this

  • Turn Δ up from zero and follow the cone opening into a gap. Note the effective mass m* = Δ / v_F² appearing at K.
  • Compare the DOS at E = 0 for graphene (zero) and for a metal (finite).
  • Dope graphene with the n-doped and p-doped presets and read the Fermi energy. Compare it with the Dirac-cone estimate, then keep raising the electron count. At 2.5 electrons per cell E_F reaches the van Hove peak at E = t, the Fermi contour touches the zone edge at M, and the Dirac-cone estimate fails.
  • Dope the h-BN-like case. As soon as the electron count differs from 2, E_F jumps from midgap to a band edge and the material conducts. The energy price of about half the gap is why doping a wide-gap insulator is hard in practice.

s-band along Γ – X – K – Γ – L

DOS (states / eV / atom)

What is going on

This is the three-dimensional version of Tab 1, for an s-orbital on an fcc lattice. Summing over the 12 nearest neighbours at R = (a/2)(±1, ±1, 0) and permutations (HW6) gives the dispersion of HW5, Q4:

E(k) = E_s − β − 4γ [ cos(kₓa/2)cos(k_ya/2) + cos(k_ya/2)cos(k_za/2) + cos(k_za/2)cos(kₓa/2) ]

  • The band minimum is at Γ, E_s − β − 12γ, and the maximum at X, E_s − β + 4γ, so the bandwidth is 16γ (HW5 4c).
  • The DOS is computed by sampling the Brillouin zone with half a million random k-points. The kinks and steps are 3D van Hove singularities.
  • The X–K segment is drawn with its true length in k-space.

Try this

  • Verify the reduced dispersions of HW5 4a: along Γ–X and along Γ–L.
  • Fill the band with 1 electron per atom and read off E_F. Then change γ and see how the effective mass at Γ, m* = ħ²/(2γa²), responds.

Bands. Cyan: s-like, orange: p-like

DOS (states / eV / atom)

What is going on

A group 2 atom has two valence electrons, which completely fill the ns level. A chain built from this one orbital is an insulator (Tab 1 with 2 electrons per atom). The empty np level lies an energy ε_p above it (for Be, ε₂ₛ = 0 and ε₃ₚ ≈ 3.95 eV). With both orbitals on every site the Bloch Hamiltonian is a 2×2 matrix (booklet footnote 26, HW6 Q3):

H(k) = [ a(k), 2i t_sp sin ka ; −2i t_sp sin ka, b(k) ]

a = −2t_ss cos ka, b = ε_p + 2σ t_pp cos ka, E±(k) = (a+b)/2 ± √( ((a−b)/2)² + (2t_sp sin ka)² )

  • Decoupled orbitals (t_sp = 0), as in HW6: an s-band of width 4t_ss and a p-band of width 4t_pp. They overlap in energy once ε_p < 2(t_ss + t_pp), which for Be means t_eff = t_ss + t_pp > 1.98 eV. Then the two electrons spill into both bands and the solid is a (semi)metal.
  • The sign σ decides where the p-band has its minimum. For p orbitals pointing along the chain the neighbouring lobes face each other with opposite signs, so the p-band has its minimum at the zone edge, right where the s-band has its maximum: a direct gap. With the opposite sign the minimum sits at Γ: an indirect gap (HW6 Q3d, Figures A and B).
  • If s–p hopping is switched on, the crossing becomes an avoided crossing and the colour along the bands shows the mixed orbital character. In this 1D model a hybridisation gap opens. In a 3D crystal the s-like and p-like states can overlap at different points in k-space, so a gap does not have to open and semimetallic behaviour survives (HW6 Q3d).
  • The compression slider scales all hoppings as 1/a², an illustrative Harrison-type rule. It shows how squeezing a chain drives the insulator into a metal.

Try this

  • Start from the Be-like preset (t_ss = t_pp = 0.8 eV). Raise t_pp until the gap closes. Compare the value of t_ss + t_pp to ε_p/2.
  • Flip the sign σ and repeat. The gap type changes from direct to indirect, and the bands can overlap even though no vertical transition is possible.
  • Switch on t_sp in the semimetal case. The crossing is avoided and a gap opens. Why can this not happen for decoupled orbitals?
